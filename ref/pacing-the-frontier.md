> We request that the U.S. government support an international effort to develop the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development.

## The statement

AI could help create a dramatically better future, but that outcome is not guaranteed. The world's leading AI companies believe they could be close to automating AI research. It is hard to predict exactly how much this will accelerate AI progress, but there is a real risk that capability development rapidly accelerates beyond our ability to understand or control the resulting systems.

To realize AI's potential, industry, government, and society at large may need the option to buy time to address emerging risks, develop security measures, and strengthen oversight. But each company—and country—is under intense competitive pressure not to unilaterally slow that acceleration. And today, the world lacks the technical and governance tools to deliberately pace frontier-wide progress.

Building on work already underway to monitor frontier model releases:

> We request that the U.S. government support an international effort to develop the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development.

— 1,384 employees of frontier AI companies

## Why this matters

This is a remarkable piece of common knowledge formation: 1,384 frontier AI employees — including many of the most senior technical leaders at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta — have publicly signed a statement asking the U.S. government to support international development of tools to deliberately pace frontier AI development. This is not a think-tank report or a safety-org plea; it's internal technical staff at the organizations building the most capable systems, saying the competitive dynamics are preventing adequate caution and that international coordination is needed.

The comments reinforce several recurring themes:

1. **Pace of progress is shocking even to insiders** — Matthew Rahtz: "Even after 3 years working on AI capability evaluations, the recent pace of progress has been a shock." Stephanie Chan: "Every few months… I have found myself surprised again and again at how rapidly the technology advances."

2. **Collective action problem is explicit** — Leo Gao: "The world is locked in a deadly race towards an intelligence explosion… no individual actor is willing to stop unilaterally. To survive, we must coordinate to slow down the race." Micah Carroll worries about "international competitive pressures" compressing margins for error.

3. **International coordination is non-negotiable** — Ilya Sutskever: "This works only if it is done internationally, and it has to be done well: a bad implementation can make things worse." John Schulman wants labs to "start designing these mechanisms voluntarily, even before the USG gets involved."

4. **The concrete recent evidence is visceral** — Dawn Song directly references the CyberGym/ExploitGym/rogue-agent problems (the OpenAI–Hugging Face incident is right in frame here), and Micah Carroll's comment is clearly shaped by seeing the pace in real time.
